## Is renting better than buying?

**Renting is better than buying when three things line up: homes are expensive relative to rents, you might move within roughly five years, and you invest the money that buying would have tied up.** In that situation a renter usually finishes with more net worth than an owner, with less risk concentrated in one asset. If any of the three conditions fails, the case weakens fast.

Asking "is renting better than buying" is really asking which choice leaves you wealthier and more secure at the end of a realistic time frame. Housing is a cost either way. This guide sets out the strongest honest case for renting, puts numbers on it, and then shows where it stops being true.

## Why renting is better than owning in many markets

### Owners have unrecoverable costs too

The phrase "rent is dead money" ignores the owner's own dead money. An owner pays mortgage interest, property tax, maintenance, insurance and, in apartments, service charges or HOA fees. None of that builds equity. Then there are buying costs on the way in and selling costs on the way out.

Take our US default: a $420,000 home, 20% down, a 6.3% 30-year mortgage, 1.1% property tax, 1% maintenance and 0.55% insurance. In the first month the owner's total outflow is about **$3,007**, against a renter's **$2,115** for a comparable home (rent plus renter's insurance). Of the $2,080 mortgage payment, roughly $1,764 is interest in month one. Add tax, maintenance and insurance and the owner's unrecoverable cost is close to $2,690 a month, higher than the rent.

### The down payment has a job to do

A purchase also absorbs a large lump sum. In the US example the buyer needs **$96,600** on day one: $84,000 down plus about $12,600 in buying costs. A renter can invest that money. At a 7% after-tax return, $96,600 becomes roughly $190,000 in ten years before adding any monthly savings. This is the opportunity cost explained in our guide to the [opportunity cost of a down payment](/blog/opportunity-cost-of-a-down-payment/).

### Flexibility has real money value

Renters can move for a better job, a relationship or a cheaper neighbourhood without paying 5–7% in agent and transfer costs. In many countries a move also avoids a second round of stamp duty. For people early in their careers, the ability to relocate for higher pay can be worth more than any housing return.

### Less risk in one place

A home is a single, leveraged, illiquid asset in one city. With 20% down, a 10% fall in prices wipes out half your equity. A renter's invested savings can be spread across thousands of companies and countries and sold in a day.

## When is renting better than buying? The worked numbers

We ran our calculator's market defaults over a 10-year horizon. Each row assumes the renter invests the down payment, the buying costs and any monthly difference at the market's default return.

| Market (defaults) | Price | Rent / month | Price-to-rent | Buyer net worth, yr 10 | Renter net worth, yr 10 | Who wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | $420,000 | $2,100 | 16.7 | $273,500 | $314,900 | Rent by ~$41,000 |
| New York City | $850,000 | $4,200 | 16.9 | $488,800 | $736,000 | Rent by ~$247,000 |
| Toronto | C$1,050,000 | C$2,700 | 32.4 | C$668,700 | C$1,032,400 | Rent by ~C$364,000 |
| Hong Kong | HK$8,000,000 | HK$20,000 | 33.3 | HK$4.41m | HK$6.63m | Rent by ~HK$2.2m |
| Sydney | A$1,450,000 | A$3,460 | 34.9 | A$1.35m | A$1.45m | Rent by ~A$102,000 |
| Germany | €450,000 | €1,500 | 25.0 | €252,400 | €317,100 | Rent by ~€65,000 |

The pattern is consistent. Where prices are 25 to 35 times annual rent, as in Toronto, Hong Kong and Sydney, renting and investing pulls clearly ahead. New York wins for renters even at a lower ratio because co-op and condo charges ($1,200 a month in our defaults) and 7% selling costs pile onto the owner. Germany's 10% purchase costs (Kaufnebenkosten) are a heavy upfront hurdle.

Under the US defaults, buying never catches up within 30 years. The gap stays between about $39,000 and $66,000 in the renter's favour at every horizon we tested, from 3 to 30 years. That is not a law of nature. It reflects a 6.3% mortgage rate, 3.5% price growth and a 7% investment return. Change those and the answer moves, which is why you should run your own numbers in the [rent vs own calculator](/rent-vs-own-calculator/).

### The break-even rent tells you where the line is

Our calculator reports a break-even rent: the monthly rent at which both paths finish level at your horizon. For the US defaults over ten years it is about **$2,310**. If you can rent a comparable home for less than that, renting is better on these assumptions. At three years the break-even rent rises to about $3,040, because buying and selling costs have less time to be absorbed. Short horizons strongly favour renting.

## Is it better to rent than buy if you won't invest?

This is the condition that matters most, so it deserves its own section.

The renter's lead in every table above comes from investing. If the renter keeps the money in a low-yield account or spends it, the result reverses. Re-running the US defaults with a lower return on the renter's savings:

| Renter's after-tax return | Buyer net worth, yr 10 | Renter net worth, yr 10 | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7% (default) | $273,500 | $314,900 | Rent ahead by ~$41,000 |
| 4% | $273,500 | $248,500 | Buy ahead by ~$25,000 |
| 3% | $273,500 | $229,600 | Buy ahead by ~$44,000 |
| ~0% (cash) | $273,500 | $181,200 | Buy ahead by ~$92,000 |

The tipping point sits somewhere between a 4% and 7% return in this example. A renter who would leave savings in cash is better off buying, because the mortgage forces saving. The honest case for renting is therefore a case for **renting and investing**, which we cover in depth in [renting and investing vs buying a house](/blog/renting-and-investing-vs-buying-a-house/).

## Why is renting better than owning for some people but not others?

Renting is the better default for:

- **People likely to move within five years.** Transaction costs dominate short holds.
- **High price-to-rent markets.** When a home costs more than about 20–25 times annual rent, renters usually have a cost advantage. See our [price-to-rent ratio guide](/blog/price-to-rent-ratio/).
- **Disciplined investors.** Anyone who automatically invests the difference each month captures the full benefit.
- **People in uncertain jobs or relationships.** Selling a home under pressure is expensive.
- **Buyers facing high rates.** A higher mortgage rate raises the owner's interest bill and lifts the break-even rent.

Renting is weaker for:

- **Long-horizon households in low price-to-rent markets.** Our UK defaults (price-to-rent 17.9) show buying ahead by around £106,000 after ten years.
- **People who would not invest the savings.**
- **Markets with fast rent growth and weak tenant protection,** where the renter's housing cost is less predictable.
- **Anyone who values control:** renovating, keeping pets, staying put indefinitely.

## The risks of renting you should not ignore

A fair case for renting has to admit its weak points:

1. **Rent rises.** Owners with long fixed-rate mortgages lock most of their housing cost; renters do not. Rent growth of 4% a year doubles rent in about 18 years.
2. **Security of tenure.** Tenant protections vary widely. Germany and much of continental Europe default to open-ended contracts; many US leases run 12 months at a time.
3. **Behaviour.** Investing the difference takes discipline every month, through market falls.
4. **Retirement housing costs.** An owner with a paid-off home has very low housing costs later in life. A renter needs a portfolio large enough to cover rising rent.

None of these makes renting wrong. They mean the renter should hold a larger cash buffer and automate investing.

## A five-step check: is renting better for you?

1. **Estimate your horizon honestly.** If it is under five years, renting is the default.
2. **Run the 5% rule.** Multiply the price by 5% and divide by 12. On a $420,000 home that is $1,750. Rent below it hints at renting; rent above hints at buying. Our [5% rule guide](/blog/5-percent-rule-rent-vs-buy/) explains the limits.
3. **Find your break-even rent** in the calculator with your real rate, taxes and fees.
4. **Decide what happens to the savings.** If you will not invest them automatically, assume a low return.
5. **Stress-test.** Lower price growth by 1 point and raise rent growth by 1 point. If renting still wins, the case is robust.

## The bottom line on renting vs buying

Renting is better than buying more often than conventional wisdom suggests, especially in expensive cities and over short horizons. The advantage is real but conditional: it belongs to renters who invest what they do not spend on housing. If that describes you, the numbers in many markets are on your side. If it does not, owning's built-in saving may serve you better. For the opposite argument, read [why buying a house is better than renting](/blog/why-buying-a-house-is-better-than-renting/).
