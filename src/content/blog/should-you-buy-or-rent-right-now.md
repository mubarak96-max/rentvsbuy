## Is it better to rent or buy right now?

**As of November 2026, renting looks relatively more attractive than it did a year ago in most large markets, because mortgage rates have risen again while price growth has slowed. Buying still makes sense where prices are moderate relative to rents and you plan to stay for many years.** The right answer depends far more on your horizon, your local price-to-rent ratio and your quoted rate than on where rates go next.

Here is what has changed, what it does to the numbers, and how to decide without trying to time the market.

## What changed in late 2026

### Mortgage rates have turned higher again

| Market | Latest move | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Freddie Mac 30-year fixed average up from a year earlier | **7.18%** on 5 Nov 2026 (6.22% a year earlier) |
| United Kingdom | Bank of England raised Bank Rate 0.25 points on 5 Nov 2026 | Bank Rate **4%**; average two-year fix about **6%** in early October (Moneyfacts) |
| Canada | Bank of Canada raised its policy rate on 28 Oct 2026, its first hike in over three years | Policy rate **2.5%** |
| Australia | RBA raised the cash rate on 3 Nov 2026, its fifth increase this year | Cash rate **4.85%**, highest since 2008 |
| Eurozone | ECB held on 29 Oct 2026 after raising in September | Deposit rate **2.50%** |

Sources: [Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms), [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/the-interest-rate-bank-rate), Bank of Canada, [Reserve Bank of Australia Statement on Monetary Policy](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/smp/2026/nov/overview.html), and the [European Central Bank](https://ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html). Moneyfacts reported the UK average two-year fixed rate at 5.98% on 5 October 2026, its highest since December 2023.

The common thread is inflation pressure, which several central banks have linked to energy prices and conflict in the Middle East. That matters for buyers because it means rates could stay elevated for longer than markets expected at the start of the year.

### Price growth is modest

In the US, the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller National Index rose **2.1%** in the year to August 2026, while consumer prices rose 3.4%, so real home prices dipped slightly. Higher rates tend to cool price growth because they shrink how much buyers can borrow. That is not the same as prices falling, and local markets differ.

## Should I buy or rent right now? What higher rates do to the numbers

We re-ran our calculator's market defaults over ten years with mortgage rates closer to what borrowers are being quoted in late 2026. The renter invests the down payment, buying costs and monthly difference in every case.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | 6.3% | Rent ahead ~$41,000 | 7.18% | Rent ahead ~$81,000 |
| United Kingdom | 4.3% | Buy ahead ~£106,000 | 5.9% | Buy ahead ~£56,000 |
| Australia | 5.6% | Buy ahead ~A$17,000 | 6.6% | Rent ahead ~A$78,000 |
| Canada | 4.4% | Rent ahead ~C$97,000 | 4.9% | Rent ahead ~C$131,000 |
| Germany | 3.6% | Rent ahead ~€65,000 | 4.1% | Rent ahead ~€86,000 |
| Ireland | 3.8% | Buy ahead ~€283,000 | 4.3% | Buy ahead ~€262,000 |

The test rates for Australia, Canada, Germany and Ireland are illustrative increases of 0.5–1 point, not quoted averages. Rates for your loan depend on your deposit, credit and lender.

Three lessons stand out:

1. **Rates move the verdict.** In Australia, a one-point rise flips our default from buying to renting.
2. **Strong buying cases survive.** The UK and Ireland defaults remain clearly in favour of buying, though the UK lead roughly halves.
3. **Break-even rents rise.** The US break-even rent climbs from about $2,310 to about $2,512 a month. If you can rent a comparable home for less than that, renting wins on these assumptions.

Our model keeps the rate fixed for the whole loan. In the UK, Canada and Australia, most borrowers fix for a few years and then refinance, so the long-run cost could be higher or lower. Read more in [how interest rates affect rent vs buy](/blog/how-interest-rates-affect-rent-vs-buy/).

## What higher rates mean for a monthly budget

Headline rates translate directly into payments. On the US default loan of $336,000 over 30 years:

| Rate | Monthly principal and interest | Difference vs 6.3% |
|---|---|---|
| 6.3% | ~$2,080 | – |
| 6.75% | ~$2,179 | +$99 |
| 7.18% | ~$2,276 | +$196 |
| 7.5% | ~$2,349 | +$269 |

A rise of under one point adds nearly $200 a month, or about $2,350 a year, all of it interest. For a UK borrower with a £261,000 loan over 25 years, moving from 4.3% to 5.9% lifts the payment from about £1,421 to about £1,666 a month. That extra cost is why break-even rents climb and why some buyers who qualified a year ago no longer do.

## Why timing the market is so hard

It is tempting to wait for "the bottom". Here is why that rarely works out as planned.

- **Rates and prices often move in opposite directions.** When rates fall, demand returns and prices can rise. A lower rate on a higher price may not be cheaper.
- **Waiting costs rent.** Every month you wait, you pay rent. That is fine if renting makes sense for you, but it is a cost of waiting.
- **Rate cycles turn without warning.** The Bank of Canada's October 2026 increase was its first in more than three years, and the RBA has raised rates five times this year. Few buyers planning around cuts a year earlier would have predicted that path.
- **Your life does not wait.** Job moves, children and school catchments rarely line up with the rate cycle.
- **Refinancing is an option, not a guarantee.** If rates fall, you may be able to refinance, but there are fees, and you must still qualify.

A better approach is to make a decision that works under a range of rate paths.

## Should you rent or buy a house right now? A decision framework

### Buying makes more sense now if:

- You expect to stay at least seven to ten years.
- The monthly cost at today's rate is affordable with a buffer, ideally under about a third of take-home pay.
- Local prices are under roughly 20 times annual rent for a comparable home. See our [price-to-rent ratio guide](/blog/price-to-rent-ratio/).
- You could cope if your rate rose another point at your next renewal.

### Renting makes more sense now if:

- You may move within five years.
- Your quoted rate pushes the break-even rent well above what comparable homes rent for.
- You are stretching to the lender's maximum to buy.
- You will invest the money you would have put into a deposit.

### Either way, prepare for both paths

- Keep saving the deposit in a safe, accessible account if you plan to buy within two or three years.
- Re-run your numbers every few months with your actual quoted rate.
- Get a mortgage agreement in principle so you can move quickly if the right home appears.

## How to run your own right-now check

1. Get a real rate quote, not a headline average.
2. Enter it in the [rent vs mortgage calculator](/rent-vs-mortgage-calculator/) with your price, rent and local costs.
3. Run it at your quoted rate, at one point higher and at one point lower.
4. Look at the break-even year. If buying only wins after more years than you plan to stay, rent.
5. Compare the break-even rent with actual local rents.

## The bottom line

Is it better to buy or rent right now? Higher rates in late 2026 have tilted the maths toward renting in the US, Canada and Australia, while buying remains attractive in lower price-to-rent markets such as the UK and Ireland. Base your choice on how long you will stay and what your numbers say at today's rate, not on predicting next year's.
