## Rent to own vs mortgage: which is better?

**For most people who can qualify, a mortgage is cheaper and safer than rent to own.** A rent-to-own agreement typically charges an upfront option fee and above-market rent, and you can lose both if you miss a payment, the home turns out to need major repairs, or you cannot get a mortgage when the option runs out. Rent to own can make sense as a short bridge for someone who is close to qualifying, but only with fair terms, an independent inspection and a lawyer's review.

Rent to own is a broad label. It covers several different contracts with very different risks, so the first job is to know which one you are being offered.

## Lease to own vs rent to own: the contract types

"Rent to own" and "lease to own" are used interchangeably. The meaningful difference is between the following structures.

| Structure | What you sign | Do you have to buy? | Who holds legal title during the term | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lease-option | A lease plus an option to buy at a set price | No, it's your choice | Seller | Lose the option fee and credits if you don't buy |
| Lease-purchase | A lease plus a contract to buy | Yes, you're obliged to | Seller | Breach of contract if you can't complete |
| Contract for deed (land contract) | An instalment sale with seller financing | You are already buying | Seller, until the final payment | Losing the home and all payments on default |
| Traditional mortgage | Purchase financed by a lender | You own from day one | You (lender holds a lien) | Foreclosure, with legal protections |

**A lease-option** is a rental agreement with a separate option giving you the right, but not the obligation, to buy the home at an agreed price by a deadline. You pay an option fee for that right.

**A lease-purchase** commits you to buy at the end of the lease. If you cannot get financing, you may lose your money and face a breach claim.

**A contract for deed** is not a lease at all. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes these as loans where the seller keeps legal title until the buyer makes all the payments, while the buyer typically pays property taxes, insurance and repairs. The CFPB's [report on contract for deed lending](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/report-on-contract-for-deed-lending/) found that sellers often do not assess whether buyers can repay, and that buyers can lose a home far more abruptly than under a mortgage foreclosure. In August 2024 the CFPB also issued an advisory opinion saying these arrangements are generally consumer credit under federal lending law; check the CFPB site for its current status.

## How much does rent to own cost? A worked example

Consider a $300,000 US home that would rent for $1,900 a month on the open market. A rent-to-own provider offers:

- Option fee: 3% of the price, **$9,000**, credited toward purchase.
- Monthly rent: $2,150, of which **$250** is a rent credit toward purchase.
- Term: three years.
- Purchase price locked at **$318,000** (about 2% a year above today's value).

After three years the tenant has $9,000 + ($250 × 36) = **$18,000** in credits. That money was paid on top of market rent and is only worth something if the purchase goes through. What happens next depends on the market and on whether the tenant can get a mortgage.

| Outcome after 3 years | Home value | If tenant buys | If tenant walks away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prices rise 3.5% a year | ~$332,600 | Pays $300,000 net of credits; ~$14,600 gain over the $18,000 premium | Loses $18,000 and the gain |
| Prices flat | $300,000 | Pays $300,000 net; premium roughly recovered, no gain | Loses $18,000 |
| Prices fall 5% | $285,000 | Pays $300,000 net for a $285,000 home | Loses $18,000 |
| Tenant can't get a mortgage | Any | Not possible | Loses $18,000 |

In three of the four outcomes the tenant ends up no better off than an ordinary renter, and often worse.

### Rent to own vs buying now with a mortgage

Compare a buyer who takes a mortgage on day one with a 10% deposit ($30,000), a 7% 30-year fixed rate and around 3% in closing costs. The monthly mortgage payment is about **$1,796**; with 1.1% property tax, 1% maintenance and 0.55% insurance, the total monthly cost is about **$2,459**. That is about $309 more than the rent-to-own payment, but after three years of 3.5% price growth the buyer's equity is roughly **$71,000** (home value minus a remaining balance of about $261,000), for $39,000 paid upfront. The buyer also captured all the appreciation, rather than a pre-agreed slice of it.

This is the core of rent to own vs financing: the mortgage borrower pays a lender for money, at a regulated, disclosed interest rate. The rent-to-own tenant pays a seller for an option, at an implicit price that is rarely disclosed as a rate at all.

## Contract to deed vs rent to own: which is riskier?

Both carry more risk than a mortgage, but in different ways.

- **Lease-option:** your maximum loss is usually the option fee plus rent credits. You can walk away.
- **Contract for deed:** you may have paid years of principal, taxes and repairs, yet without legal title. In some states a missed payment can trigger forfeiture rather than foreclosure. Some states require foreclosure-style protections; many do not.

New York's Department of Financial Services has warned that rent-to-own and land instalment deals are often marketed to financially distressed consumers, frequently involve severely distressed properties, and may shift repair costs that New York law would generally place on landlords. Its [consumer guidance on rent-to-own and land instalment contracts](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/help_for_homeowners/rent-to-own_and_land_installment_contracts) suggests considering whether a traditional lease is a better option, and having documents reviewed by an attorney.

## Red flags before you sign

- The price is set well above a recent independent appraisal.
- You are asked to waive an inspection, or the property has been vacant with utilities off.
- Repairs are your responsibility, but the seller keeps title.
- One late payment voids your option and credits.
- The seller has a mortgage on the property and you have no proof it is being paid.
- The contract is not recorded with the county.
- You cannot get a clear answer on how a lender would treat your credits.

## Is rent to own better than buying? When it can work

Rent to own can be reasonable when:

- You are close to mortgage-ready and have a specific fix in progress (for example, a new job that needs a few months of history).
- The price is fair, confirmed by an independent appraisal.
- The option fee is modest and the contract gives a reasonable grace period on late rent.
- The seller is the legal owner with clear title, confirmed by a title search.
- A lawyer has reviewed the contract, and a lender has confirmed how the credits will count.

If you are more than a year or two from qualifying, renting normally and saving the premium yourself is usually safer. The $18,000 in our example, saved in an account you control, keeps its value whether or not you buy that particular house.

## UK Rent to Buy is a different product

In England, **Rent to Buy** is a government-backed affordable housing route, not a private option contract. According to the GOV.UK [Capital Funding Guide](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/capital-funding-guide/2-rent-to-buy), Rent to Buy homes are let at an intermediate rent that must not exceed 80% of current market rent, including service charges, for a discounted period of at least five years, so the tenant can save for a deposit. There is no option fee and no pre-agreed price. The tenant may later buy the home outright, buy it through shared ownership, or move on. It is closer in spirit to discounted renting than to a US lease-option. Our guide to [shared ownership vs renting](/blog/shared-ownership-vs-renting/) covers the related UK scheme.

## A note on rent to own vs financing for goods

The same phrase is used for furniture, appliances and electronics. The logic is similar: rent-to-own stores let you take the item now and own it after a series of payments, with the total often far above the retail price. Standard financing or saving up usually costs much less. If you are weighing rent-to-own goods, compare the total of all payments with the cash price.

## The bottom line

Rent to own vs mortgage is not a close call for people who can qualify for a loan: the mortgage is cheaper, more transparent and better protected by law. Rent to own is a high-cost bridge. Use it only if the bridge is short, the terms are fair, and you have independent professionals checking the home and the contract.
