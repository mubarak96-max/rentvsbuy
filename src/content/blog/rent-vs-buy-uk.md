## Is it better to rent or buy in the UK?

On renting vs buying UK numbers in late 2026, **buying is usually the better financial choice if you plan to stay at least five years, and often after only two or three**, because UK rents are high relative to prices, first-time buyers pay little or no Stamp Duty, and transaction costs are lower than in much of Europe. **In London the advantage narrows**: break-even takes longer, and buying can lose to renting if prices grow slowly or you move within a few years.

The rest of this guide shows the numbers, the 2025–2026 rule changes that matter, and where the answer flips.

## What does buying cost in the UK?

### Stamp Duty Land Tax from April 2025

In England and Northern Ireland, residential Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) rates since 1 April 2025 are, according to [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates):

| Portion of price | Standard rate | First-time buyer rate |
|---|---|---|
| Up to £125,000 | 0% | 0% (up to £300,000) |
| £125,001–£250,000 | 2% | 0% (up to £300,000) |
| £250,001–£300,000 | 5% | 0% |
| £300,001–£500,000 | 5% | 5% |
| £500,001–£925,000 | 5% | No relief: standard rates apply to whole price |
| £925,001–£1.5 million | 10% | No relief |
| Above £1.5 million | 12% | No relief |

A 5% surcharge applies if the purchase leaves you owning more than one residential property, and non-UK residents pay an extra 2%. Scotland (LBTT) and Wales (LTT) set their own rates.

What that means in practice:

| Purchase | First-time buyer SDLT | Other buyer SDLT |
|---|---|---|
| £290,000 (UK default) | £0 | £4,500 |
| £400,000 | £5,000 | £10,000 |
| £500,000 | £10,000 | £15,000 |
| £550,000 (London default) | £17,500 (no relief) | £17,500 |

The cliff at £500,000 is why many London first-time buyers aim just below it.

### Other costs

Add conveyancing, searches, a survey and lender fees, typically a few thousand pounds. Our UK default sets total buying costs at 2.5% of the price, London at 4% to reflect SDLT. Owners also pay maintenance (default 1% of value a year) and buildings insurance; leasehold flats add service charges and sometimes ground rent.

### Mortgage rates and the refinancing cycle

UK mortgages are typically fixed for two or five years, then remortgaged. The Bank of England's Bank Rate stood at 3.75% in September 2026, and our default mortgage rate is 4.3%. The risk is at the end of each fix: on our UK default loan of £261,000, the balance after a five-year fix at 4.3% is about £228,500. Remortgaging at 5.3% adds about £125 a month; at 6.3%, about £256 a month.

## What does renting cost in the UK, and what changed in 2026?

Renters pay rent, contents insurance and usually a deposit capped at five weeks' rent (six weeks above £50,000 a year) in England. Council tax is paid by renters and owners alike, so it cancels out.

### The Renters' Rights Act 2025

The Renters' Rights Act received Royal Assent in October 2025, and its main tenancy reforms took effect in England on **1 May 2026**:

- **Section 21 "no-fault" evictions are abolished.** Landlords need a legal ground, such as selling or moving in, to regain possession.
- **Tenancies are periodic** rather than fixed-term, so tenants can leave with two months' notice.
- **Rent increases are limited to once a year** using a formal notice, and tenants can challenge above-market rises at tribunal.
- **Rental bidding wars are banned**, and landlords cannot unreasonably refuse a request to keep a pet.

These changes make renting in England more secure, which reduces one of the strongest non-financial arguments for buying. They may also lead some landlords to sell, which could affect rent levels; our default UK rent growth of 4% a year is an assumption you can change.

## Worked example: the UK average and London

### UK defaults

Using the [UK rent vs buy calculator](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/united-kingdom/): a £290,000 home, £1,350 rent, 10% deposit, a 4.3% mortgage over 25 years, 3% annual price growth, 4% rent growth and a 6% after-tax return.

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Upfront cash (deposit + costs) | £36,250 |
| Monthly mortgage payment | £1,421 |
| First-month all-in cost to own | £1,699 (vs £1,360 to rent) |
| Break-even year | Year 2 |
| Buy advantage after 5 years | about £34,900 |
| Buy advantage after 10 years | about £106,400 |
| Break-even rent (10 years) | about £794 a month |

Buying wins quickly because the deposit is small (so the renter has little to invest), first-time buyer Stamp Duty is nil, and 10% leverage magnifies 3% price growth into a much larger return on equity. The renter would need a comparable home for under about £800 a month to come out ahead.

### London defaults

Using the London defaults: a £550,000 flat, £2,300 rent, 10% deposit, 4.3% over 30 years, a £150 monthly service charge, 4% buying costs, and 2.5% annual price growth.

| Scenario | Buy advantage after 10 years | Break-even year |
|---|---|---|
| Defaults | about £98,600 | Year 4 |
| Sell after 5 years | about £18,200 | Year 4 |
| Price growth 2% | about £67,400 | Year 5 |
| Price growth 1% | about £9,000 | Year 10 |
| Mortgage rate 5.5% | about £25,300 | Year 8 |

London buying still wins at the defaults, but the margin is far more sensitive. A combination of slow price growth and higher rates can push break-even beyond the time many Londoners stay in one flat. ## Schemes and reforms that change the maths

### Lifetime ISA

A Lifetime ISA lets savers aged 18–39 pay in up to £4,000 a year and receive a 25% government bonus, up to £1,000 a year, toward a first home costing **£450,000 or less**. Withdrawals for other purposes normally lose 25%, which wipes out the bonus and some of your own money. The £450,000 cap has not risen in line with prices, which limits its use in London. The government has announced plans to replace it with a new first-time buyer ISA later this decade; existing accounts can continue.

### Shared ownership

Shared ownership lets you buy a 10–75% share and pay rent on the rest. It can lower the deposit, but you pay all maintenance and service charges on the whole home, and rents on the unowned share rise each year. Model it in the [shared ownership calculator](/shared-ownership-calculator/) and compare against renting with our [shared ownership vs renting guide](/blog/shared-ownership-vs-renting/).

### Leasehold reform

Most flats in England and Wales are leasehold. The Leasehold Reform (Ground Rent) Act 2022 set ground rent on most new long residential leases to a peppercorn. The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 aims to make lease extensions and freehold purchases cheaper, though much of it still depends on secondary legislation. In January 2026 the government published a draft Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill proposing to cap existing ground rents at £250 a year and make commonhold the default for new flats. Treat these as direction of travel: check the lease length, ground rent and service charge history before buying a flat.

## Should you rent or buy in London or elsewhere in the UK?

Lean towards **buying** if:

- You expect to stay at least five years (London) or three years (most other regions).
- You qualify for first-time buyer relief and can keep the price under £500,000.
- You can fix your rate for five years and still afford a 2-point rise at remortgage.
- The flat has a long lease and a reasonable, stable service charge.

Lean towards **renting** if:

- You may relocate within a few years for work or family.
- The only homes you can afford are short-lease flats or have uncertain service charges.
- You would stretch to a 5% deposit and have no emergency fund left.
- London prices in your area have been flat and you have no strong view that will change.
