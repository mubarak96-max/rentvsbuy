## Is it better to rent or buy in different cities and countries?

Comparing rent vs buy by city shows that **the answer depends more on local prices, rents and interest rates than on any universal rule**. In our market presets, buying wins over 10 years where rental yields are high relative to mortgage rates, such as Ireland, the UK, Dubai, Portugal and Japan. Renting wins where homes are expensive relative to rents or borrowing is costly, such as Toronto, Sydney, Hong Kong, India and Brazil.

The figures below come from the default assumptions in our [rent vs buy calculator](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/), run over a 10-year horizon. They are **illustrative starting points, not forecasts or official averages**. Prices and rents vary widely within every country, so the useful lesson is the pattern, not any single figure.

## Two numbers that explain most of the difference

**The price-to-rent ratio** is a home's price divided by a year's rent for a comparable home. A ratio of 20 means the home costs 20 years of rent. Higher ratios generally favour renting. Our [price-to-rent ratio guide](/blog/price-to-rent-ratio/) covers how to calculate it for a specific property.

**Gross rental yield** is the inverse: annual rent divided by price. A 5% yield means rent equals 5% of the home's value each year.

The most revealing quick test is to compare **gross yield with the mortgage rate**. If rent is a bigger percentage of the price than the interest rate, renting is relatively expensive and buying tends to win. If the mortgage rate is higher than the yield, buying is costly relative to renting, and it needs strong price growth to compensate.

## Why does the answer differ so much between countries?

### 1. Interest rates relative to rents

The yield-minus-rate column predicts most verdicts. Japan, Ireland, Dubai, Spain and Portugal all have rents well above their mortgage rates, and all favour buying. Brazil, Mexico and India sit at the other extreme, where mortgages cost 4–6 percentage points more than the rental yield. That is also why a low price-to-rent ratio does not guarantee buying wins: South Africa has the lowest ratio in our set (12.5) but a 10.5% mortgage rate, so renting is ahead for over two decades. Our article on [how interest rates affect rent vs buy](/blog/how-interest-rates-affect-rent-vs-buy/) shows this sensitivity in detail.

### 2. Investment returns available to renters

High-rate economies also tend to offer high returns on savings. In Brazil, with the Selic rate in double digits, our default after-tax return is 11%; in India, 10%. When a renter can earn that much on the deposit they did not spend, the bar for buying rises sharply. See the [opportunity cost of a down payment](/blog/opportunity-cost-of-a-down-payment/) for worked numbers.

### 3. Transaction costs

Buying costs range from about 1% in New Zealand (no stamp duty) to around 10% in Germany and 11% in Spain. High buying costs push the break-even point later, because they are lost on day one. Germany's 4% yield and 3.6% rate look neutral, but 10% Kaufnebenkosten plus modest 2% price growth tip it to renting. Spain shows the opposite: costs are high, but a 2.2-point positive spread and 4% growth still let buying win by year 5.

### 4. Expected price growth

Appreciation assumptions differ, from 1% a year in Japan and Hong Kong to 6–7% in India, Brazil and Mexico. Higher growth helps buyers, but in our defaults it is usually paired with high rates, so it rarely decides the result alone. Australia is the exception: a 5% growth assumption is what lifts it to buying by year 8 despite a negative spread.

### 5. Leverage and deposit rules

Small deposits magnify price growth. Ireland and the UK default to 10% deposits, so a 3–5% rise in the home's value is a 30–50% return on the owner's equity before costs. Hong Kong defaults to 30% down and Singapore to 25%, which dampens that effect.

### 6. Renting culture and tenant protection

Where renting is secure and long-term, people feel less pressure to buy. Germany and Switzerland have long traditions of lifetime renting with strong tenant protections and some of the lowest home-ownership rates in Europe. Rent rules also shape the numbers: Dubai links rent increases to an official index, Riyadh introduced a multi-year rent freeze in 2025, and Stockholm's regulated first-hand contracts are famously hard to get, pushing many people into buying cooperative apartments.

## How to use this comparison

1. **Find the closest market** in our [country hub](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/), such as the [United States](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/united-states/), [Australia](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/australia/) or [UAE](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/uae/).
2. **Replace the defaults** with the price and rent of an actual home and a real mortgage quote.
3. **Check the spread** between gross yield and your mortgage rate as a first signal.
4. **Set your true horizon.** Moving within five years favours renting almost everywhere.
5. **Stress-test growth** at 1–2 percentage points below the default.

If you are moving between countries, run both locations: the right choice in Dublin might be the wrong one in Toronto. This is general information, not financial advice.
