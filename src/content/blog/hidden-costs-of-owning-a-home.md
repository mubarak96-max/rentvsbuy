## What is the real cost of owning a home vs renting?

**The cost of owning a home includes far more than the mortgage: one-off buying costs (1–11% of the price), selling costs (1–7%), property tax, home insurance, maintenance of roughly 1% of the home's value a year, and HOA, strata or service charges, plus occasional special assessments. A renter's costs are mainly rent and small fees.** The owner's offset is home price growth and the principal they repay.

Most rent vs buy comparisons stop at the mortgage payment. This guide lists the costs that usually get left out, gives country-specific figures from our calculator's late-2026 defaults, and adds them up over ten years.

## The full list of homeownership costs

| Cost | When paid | Typical size | Recoverable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Down payment | At purchase | 5–30% of price | Yes, as equity (less any price fall) |
| Buying costs | At purchase | 1–11% of price | No |
| Mortgage interest | Monthly | Most of early payments | No |
| Mortgage principal | Monthly | Small at first, grows | Yes, as equity |
| Mortgage insurance | Monthly or upfront | Applies below ~20% down | No |
| Property tax | Yearly | 0–2%+ of value | No |
| Home insurance | Yearly | 0.05–0.55% of value in our defaults | No |
| Maintenance | Irregular | ~1% of value a year | No (some adds value) |
| HOA, strata, service charges | Monthly or yearly | Varies widely | No |
| Special assessments | Occasionally | Can be large | No |
| Selling costs | At sale | 1–7% of sale price | No |

## Transaction costs by country

Buying and selling costs are the biggest hidden cost of owning a home for anyone who moves within ten years. Here are the defaults our calculator uses, with what they typically cover:

| Market | Buying costs | Selling costs | Main components |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | 3% | 6% | Closing costs, lender fees; agent commission on sale |
| United Kingdom | 2.5% | 2% | Legal, survey; Stamp Duty Land Tax (0% for first-time buyers up to £300,000) |
| Canada | 2.5% | 5% | Provincial land transfer tax (plus municipal in Toronto), legal |
| Australia | 4.5% | 2.5% | State stamp duty (first-home concessions vary), conveyancing |
| UAE (Dubai) | 7% | 2% | 4% Dubai Land Department fee, ~2% agent, mortgage registration |
| India | 7% | 1% | Stamp duty and registration, usually 6–8% by state |
| Singapore | 3.5% | 2% | Progressive Buyer's Stamp Duty, legal |
| Germany | 10% | 3% | Transfer tax (3.5–6.5% by state), notary and registry (~2%), broker |
| New Zealand | 1% | 3% | No stamp duty; legal and inspection |
| Ireland | 2.5% | 2% | Stamp duty (1% up to €1 million), legal |
| France | 8% | 5% | Frais de notaire, typically 7–8% on existing homes |
| Spain | 11% | 4% | ITP transfer tax (~6–10% by region), notary, registry |

The UK stamp duty figures come from [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates); standard rates apply above £500,000 and the relief is lost entirely. Irish rates are from [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/stamp-duty/property/stamp-duty-property/rates.aspx). For anything else, check your local tax authority before buying, because thresholds and concessions change.

A buyer in Spain who sells after five years pays about 15% of the price in transaction costs. A first-time buyer in the UK pays about 4.5%. That difference alone can decide the rent vs buy question for shorter stays.

## How much should you budget for maintenance?

There is no single right figure, but three rules of thumb are widely used:

- **The 1% rule:** budget 1% of the home's value per year. On a US$420,000 home, that is US$4,200 a year or US$350 a month. Our calculator uses 1% for most markets.
- **The square-foot rule:** budget US$1 per square foot per year. A 2,000 sq ft home would need US$2,000 a year. Because it ignores the home's value, it usually gives a much lower figure than the 1% rule in expensive areas; in cheaper areas the two are closer.
- **The age adjustment:** add more for older homes. A new-build apartment may need 0.5% or less if a service charge covers the building; a 60-year-old house may need 2% or more.

| Home | 1% rule | US$1 per sq ft | Our rough planning range |
|---|---|---|---|
| New 900 sq ft condo, US$350,000 | US$3,500 | US$900 | US$1,000–2,000 plus HOA |
| 2,000 sq ft house, built 2005, US$420,000 | US$4,200 | US$2,000 | US$3,000–5,000 |
| 2,400 sq ft house, built 1965, US$600,000 | US$6,000 | US$2,400 | US$6,000–12,000 |

Changing maintenance from 1% to 1.5% for an older home moves our US default comparison from renter ahead by about US$42,000 after ten years to renter ahead by about US$77,000. It is one of the most important inputs you can personalise.

## Insurance: home vs renter's cover

Owners insure the building and contents; renters insure only contents. Our defaults set home insurance at 0.55% of value in the US, where premiums have risen sharply in disaster-prone states, compared with 0.1–0.2% in much of Europe and Canada. On the US default home, that is about US$193 a month against US$15 for a renter's policy. If you live in a wildfire, hurricane or flood zone, get a real quote before you buy.

## Service charges, HOA fees and strata levies

Apartments and many planned communities charge owners for shared costs: building insurance, lifts, cleaning, security, gardens and a reserve fund. Our defaults include:

- **Dubai:** AED 1,500 a month on a AED 1.8 million apartment.
- **New York City:** US$1,200 a month in co-op maintenance or condo common charges.
- **Singapore:** S$350 a month in condo fees.
- **India:** ₹3,000 a month in society maintenance.
- **London:** £150 a month for a leasehold flat.

These charges usually rise over time, and our calculator grows them with rent inflation. For an apartment-specific view, see [buying a condo vs renting](/blog/buying-a-condo-vs-renting/).

## Special assessments: the cost nobody budgets for

A special assessment is a one-off levy on every owner in a building or association, used when the reserve fund cannot cover a major repair. Common triggers include roofs, facades, lifts, structural repairs and new safety rules. After the 2021 Surfside condo collapse, Florida introduced milestone structural inspections and reserve-funding requirements for older condo buildings, which led to large assessments for many owners.

Before buying into any shared building, ask for:

1. The latest reserve fund study and current reserve balance.
2. Minutes from the last two years of board or strata meetings.
3. Any planned major works or pending litigation.
4. The history of fee increases.

## Mortgage insurance

If your deposit is below about 20%, expect mortgage insurance in several markets: private mortgage insurance (PMI) in the US, which can usually be cancelled once you reach 20% equity; Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) or private default insurance in Canada, added to the loan; and lenders mortgage insurance (LMI) in Australia. Our calculator does not add this automatically, so include it in your monthly costs if it applies.

## Adding it up: 10-year cost of owning vs renting

Here is the total unrecoverable cost over ten years for four markets using our defaults, before counting price growth or investment returns:

| 10-year cost | United States | United Kingdom | Germany | UAE (Dubai) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buying costs | US$12,600 | £7,250 | €45,000 | AED 126,000 |
| Mortgage interest | US$196,970 | £97,840 | €116,140 | AED 566,760 |
| Property tax | US$55,060 | £0 | €9,950 | AED 0 |
| Maintenance | US$50,060 | £33,700 | €49,720 | AED 154,030 |
| Insurance | US$27,530 | £5,060 | €4,970 | AED 11,000 |
| Service charges | US$0 | £0 | €0 | AED 226,400 |
| Selling costs | US$35,550 | £7,800 | €16,460 | AED 53,290 |
| **Owner total** | **~US$378,000** | **~£152,000** | **~€242,000** | **~AED 1.14m** |
| **Renter total (rent + fees)** | **~US$297,000** | **~£196,000** | **~€208,000** | **~AED 1.56m** |

Two things balance these totals. The owner gains price growth (about US$172,000 on the US home over ten years at 3.5% a year). The renter gains investment returns on the deposit and buying costs they did not spend. That is why the US renter, despite paying less in unrecoverable costs, needs to invest consistently to stay ahead. Our full simulation, which includes both, shows the US renter ahead by about US$42,000 after ten years, and the UK and Dubai buyers well ahead.
