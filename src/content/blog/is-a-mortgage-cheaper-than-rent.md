## Is a mortgage cheaper than rent?

**A mortgage payment can be lower than rent, but that alone does not mean owning is cheaper. Most of an early mortgage payment is interest, the payment leaves out property tax, insurance, maintenance and service charges, and buying adds one-off costs that renters never pay.** To answer the question properly, compare rent with the unrecoverable costs of owning, not with the mortgage payment.

People search "mortgage vs rent" because the two numbers look directly comparable: one cheque a month each. This guide explains why they are not, and how to make a fair rent vs mortgage comparison in a few steps.

## Mortgage vs rent: why the payments aren't comparable

Rent is a pure cost. A mortgage payment is a mix of cost (interest) and saving (principal). And it is incomplete, because owners also pay several bills a landlord would otherwise cover.

| What the cheque covers | Rent | Mortgage payment |
|---|---|---|
| Cost of using the home | Yes, all of it | Only the interest portion |
| Saving toward equity | No | Yes, the principal portion |
| Property tax | Included (landlord pays) | Not included |
| Building insurance | Included | Not included |
| Maintenance and repairs | Included | Not included |
| Strata, HOA or service charge | Usually included | Not included |
| Buying and selling costs | None | Not included, paid separately |

## How much of a mortgage payment is interest?

On an amortizing mortgage, each payment is the same, but the split changes over time. Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, so early payments are mostly interest.

Here is the split for our US default loan: US$336,000 (a US$420,000 home with 20% down) at 6.3% over 30 years, giving a payment of about **US$2,080 a month**.

| Payment number | Interest | Principal | Balance after payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | US$1,764 | US$316 | US$335,684 |
| Month 60 (year 5) | US$1,650 | US$430 | US$313,800 |
| Month 120 (year 10) | US$1,491 | US$589 | US$283,404 |
| Month 180 (year 15) | US$1,274 | US$806 | US$241,789 |
| Month 240 (year 20) | US$976 | US$1,104 | US$184,812 |

In month one, about 85% of the payment is interest. Over the full 30 years, total interest comes to about US$412,700, more than the original loan. Only about US$52,600 of the balance is repaid in the first ten years.

So when someone says "my mortgage would only be US$2,080, the same as my rent", the honest version is: "my mortgage interest would be about US$1,764, and I'd save about US$316 a month in equity". You can see the full schedule for your own loan in the [rent vs mortgage calculator](/rent-vs-mortgage-calculator/).

## The extras: what owners pay on top of the mortgage

For the US default home, the costs outside the payment in month one are:

- **Property tax (1.1% a year):** about US$385 a month. US rates vary from roughly 0.3% in Hawaii to over 2% in New Jersey and Illinois.
- **Maintenance (1% a year):** about US$350 a month, averaged over years of irregular bills.
- **Home insurance (0.55% a year):** about US$193 a month.
- **HOA dues:** zero in our default for a house, but often several hundred dollars a month for a condo.

That is about US$928 a month, or roughly 45% on top of the payment. The total first-month outflow is about US$3,007, not US$2,080. Our guide to the [hidden costs of owning a home](/blog/hidden-costs-of-owning-a-home/) covers these lines for other countries, including service charges in Dubai and strata levies in Australia.

## Mortgage payment vs rent around the world

Using our late-2026 default assumptions:

| Market | Mortgage payment | Rent | Payment vs rent | Owner's other monthly costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | US$2,080 | US$2,100 | About equal | US$928 |
| United Kingdom | £1,421 | £1,350 | 5% higher | £278 |
| Canada | C$3,081 | C$2,300 | 34% higher | C$1,167 |
| Australia | A$4,133 | A$2,800 | 48% higher | A$1,125 |
| UAE (Dubai) | AED 8,004 | AED 10,000 | 20% lower | AED 2,625 |
| India | ₹55,541 | ₹30,000 | 85% higher | ₹7,333 |
| Ireland | €1,594 | €1,900 | 16% lower | €380 |
| Spain | €932 | €1,200 | 22% lower | €453 |

In Dubai, Ireland and Spain, the mortgage is cheaper than rent before the extras. In Canada, Australia and India, it is far more expensive. The US and UK sit in the middle, where the extras decide it.

## Why is rent higher than a mortgage in some markets?

When rent is more expensive than a mortgage, there are usually clear reasons:

- **Landlords charge for all their costs.** Rent has to cover property tax, insurance, repairs, vacancies, management and a return on the landlord's own capital, not just a loan.
- **Some buyers cannot get in.** People without a deposit or credit history must rent, which supports rents.
- **Low rates relative to rents.** Where mortgage rates are low and rental yields high, as in Spain at an assumed 2.9% rate, the payment can undercut rent by a wide margin.
- **High buying costs elsewhere in the deal.** Dubai buyers pay around 7% upfront in our defaults, including the 4% Dubai Land Department transfer fee. The low monthly payment is partly paid for upfront.

A mortgage cheaper than rent is a useful signal that buying may win, but check the extras and the one-off costs before concluding.

## How interest rates change the comparison

The payment is very sensitive to the rate. For the same US$336,000, 30-year loan:

| Mortgage rate | Monthly payment | Interest in month 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 5.3% | US$1,866 | US$1,484 |
| 6.3% (our default) | US$2,080 | US$1,764 |
| 7.18% (Freddie Mac average, early Nov 2026) | US$2,276 | US$2,010 |
| 7.3% | US$2,304 | US$2,044 |

Freddie Mac's [Primary Mortgage Market Survey](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) reported an average 30-year fixed rate of 7.18% for the week of 5 November 2026. At that rate, the payment is about US$196 higher than in our default example, almost all of it extra interest. For more on this, see [how interest rates affect rent vs buy](/blog/how-interest-rates-affect-rent-vs-buy/).

A shorter term changes the mix. A 15-year loan at Freddie Mac's 15-year average of 6.57% costs about US$2,940 a month, but total interest falls to around US$193,000, less than half the 30-year figure. Paying more often can also trim interest; see [biweekly vs monthly mortgage payments](/blog/biweekly-vs-monthly-mortgage-payments/).

## Rent vs mortgage when your rate resets

In the US, most buyers fix the rate for 30 years, so the payment really is flat. In Canada, the UK and Australia, rates are typically fixed for one to five years, then reset. That means today's mortgage vs rent comparison may not hold for long.

Take our Canada default: a C$560,000 mortgage at 4.4% over 25 years costs about C$3,081 a month. After five years, about C$491,000 is still owed. If the rate at renewal is 5.4%, the payment on the remaining 20 years rises to about C$3,351. If it falls to 3.4%, the payment drops to about C$2,823. For the UK default (£261,000 at 4.3%), a reset after five years to 5.3% lifts the payment from about £1,421 to about £1,546, while 3.3% would cut it to about £1,302.

Rent can rise at renewal too, of course. But if your budget only works at today's rate, test a payment one or two points higher before you decide the mortgage is cheaper than rent.

## A fair rent vs mortgage comparison in five steps

1. **Take the interest portion** of the first payment: loan balance × annual rate ÷ 12.
2. **Add property tax, insurance, maintenance and fees** for one month.
3. **Add the lost investment return** on your deposit and buying costs: (deposit + buying costs) × expected return ÷ 12.
4. **Subtract expected price growth:** home value × a cautious growth rate ÷ 12.
5. **Compare with rent**, then remember the buying and selling costs still to come.

For the US default home, that gives roughly US$1,764 + US$928 + US$564 − US$1,225 ≈ US$2,030 a month, against US$2,115 of rent and renter's insurance. Close, before transaction costs, which is why the US example comes out slightly in the renter's favour over ten years. For the month-one versus year-ten view, read [is it cheaper to rent or buy](/blog/is-it-cheaper-to-rent-or-buy/).

## Mortgage vs rent: the bottom line

A mortgage that is cheaper than rent is a good starting signal, not a verdict. Look at the interest, add the extras, account for the deposit's lost return and spread the one-off costs over your stay.
