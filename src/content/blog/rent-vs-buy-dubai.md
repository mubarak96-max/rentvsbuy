## Should you buy or rent in Dubai?

In the rent vs buy Dubai decision, **buying usually wins if you will stay at least three to five years**, because Dubai's gross rental yields (often around 6–7%) are high, mortgage costs are moderate, and there is no annual property tax or personal capital gains tax. **Renting wins if you might leave the UAE soon, or if you expect prices to stall**, because roughly 7% of the purchase price disappears in fees on day one.

That pattern is unusual. In London, Toronto, Sydney or Hong Kong, rents are so low relative to prices that buying struggles to beat renting. Dubai's numbers look more like a high-yield market, which is why the answer here often leans the other way. The details below show where that holds and where it breaks.

## What does it cost to buy a home in Dubai?

### Upfront fees

For a ready apartment, typical buyer costs look like this. Exact amounts vary by deal and bank; check the latest fee schedule with the [Dubai Land Department](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/).

| Cost | Typical amount | On an AED 1.8M purchase with a AED 1.44M mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| DLD transfer fee | 4% of price, plus a small admin fee | AED 72,000 + admin |
| Agent commission | Usually 2% of price + 5% VAT | AED 37,800 |
| Mortgage registration | 0.25% of the loan + admin fee | about AED 3,900 |
| Registration trustee office fee | A few thousand dirhams + VAT | about AED 4,200 |
| Bank arrangement fee | Often up to 1% of the loan | up to AED 14,400 |
| Valuation | Usually a few thousand dirhams | about AED 3,000 |
| **Total** | **Roughly 7–7.5% of price** | **about AED 126,000–135,000** |

Our UAE calculator default uses 7% (AED 126,000) for buying costs.

### Mortgage rules for buyers

The Central Bank of the UAE sets maximum loan-to-value limits for mortgages. For a **first home bought by an expatriate, the cap is 80% of value up to AED 5 million** and 70% above it, meaning a minimum 20% or 30% deposit. UAE nationals can borrow somewhat more. Second homes have lower limits, and **off-plan purchases are capped at 50% loan-to-value** regardless of buyer. Banks also generally cap total debt repayments at 50% of income.

Most Dubai mortgages fix the rate for one to five years, then move to a variable rate linked to EIBOR plus a margin. That means a 25-year mortgage is exposed to rate changes over most of its life. Our default rate of 4.5% is an illustrative blended figure; get a current quote.

### Ongoing owner costs

- **Service charges:** paid annually per square foot to the owners' association and approved via the DLD's service charge system. Our default is AED 1,500 a month (AED 18,000 a year).
- **Maintenance inside the unit:** AC servicing, appliances and repairs. Our default is 0.7% of value a year.
- **Home insurance:** low cost for apartments; our default is 0.05% of value a year.
- **No annual property tax** and no personal income tax on rental income or capital gains for individuals.

## What does it cost to rent in Dubai?

- **Rent** is usually paid in one to four cheques a year, so renters need a large chunk of cash at the start of each lease.
- **Security deposit:** typically around 5% of annual rent for unfurnished homes, refundable.
- **Agent fee:** commonly around 5% of annual rent when using a broker.
- **Ejari registration:** every tenancy must be registered through the DLD's Ejari system.
- **Housing fee:** tenants pay a municipality housing fee of 5% of the annual rent, collected monthly through the DEWA bill. On AED 120,000 rent that is AED 6,000 a year, about AED 500 a month. Our default renter cost of AED 540 a month covers this fee plus contents insurance; adjust it if your rent differs.

### Rent increase rules

Dubai limits rent increases on renewal through Decree No. 43 of 2013 and the RERA rental index, which the DLD relaunched as a data-driven **Smart Rental Index** in January 2025. Landlords must give **90 days' notice** before the contract ends. The permitted increase depends on how far your current rent sits below the average for comparable homes:

| Your rent vs index average | Maximum increase |
|---|---|
| Up to 10% below | None |
| 11–20% below | 5% |
| 21–30% below | 10% |
| 31–40% below | 15% |
| More than 40% below | 20% |

This gives sitting tenants some protection, but in a rising market rents can still climb steadily, and new leases are priced at market. Our default rent growth of 5% a year reflects that.

## Worked example: buying vs renting in Dubai with our defaults

Using the [UAE rent vs buy calculator](/rent-vs-buy-calculator/uae/) defaults: an AED 1.8 million apartment, comparable rent of AED 10,000 a month, 20% down, a 4.5% mortgage over 25 years, 4% annual price growth, 5% rent growth and a 6% after-tax return on investments.

| Item | Buying | Renting |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | AED 486,000 (deposit + 7% fees) | Invested on day one |
| Monthly mortgage payment | AED 8,004 | — |
| First-month all-in cost | AED 10,629 | AED 10,540 |
| Net worth after 1 year | AED 426,500 | AED 518,000 |
| Net worth after 3 years | AED 654,000 | AED 582,000 |
| Net worth after 10 years | AED 1,830,700 | AED 875,100 |

At these assumptions **buying breaks even in year 3 and is ahead by about AED 980,000 after 10 years**. The reason is visible in the first month: owning costs almost the same as renting, because a 6.7% gross yield means rent is high relative to the price. Once the monthly costs are similar, the buyer's equity and price growth pile up while the renter's 6% return on AED 486,000 cannot keep pace.

The calculator's **break-even rent** is about AED 5,100 a month over 10 years. In other words, renting only wins on these assumptions if you could rent a comparable home for roughly half the market rent. The quick [5% rule](/5-percent-rule-calculator/) points the same way: 5% of AED 1.8M ÷ 12 is AED 7,500, below the AED 10,000 rent, which favours buying.

### When the answer flips

| Scenario (other defaults unchanged) | Result |
|---|---|
| Price growth 2% a year, 10 years | Buy ahead by about AED 514,000; break-even year 4 |
| Price growth 2%, rent growth 2% | Buy ahead by about AED 305,000; break-even year 5 |
| Flat prices (0%), 10 years | Buy ahead by about AED 145,000; break-even year 8 |
| Flat prices (0%), sell after 5 years | **Rent ahead** by about AED 105,000 |
| Prices fall 2% a year, 10 years | **Rent ahead** by about AED 162,000 |
| Mortgage rate 7% | Buy ahead by about AED 516,000; break-even year 4 |

The pattern is clear. Higher rates hurt but do not flip the answer on their own. **Short stays combined with weak prices are what make renting the better choice in Dubai.** Dubai's property market has had sharp cycles, including a significant downturn after 2014 that lasted several years, so flat or falling prices are a scenario worth taking seriously.

## Off-plan vs ready property

Off-plan homes are sold before completion, often on developer payment plans that spread the price over construction and sometimes after handover. They appeal because they need less cash early. Points to weigh:

- **Fees:** the 4% DLD fee is still due, registered through the Oqood system for off-plan units.
- **Financing:** the 50% loan-to-value cap means most buyers fund the construction phase in cash or through the payment plan.
- **Protection:** developer payments must go into a regulated escrow account, which reduces but does not remove completion risk.
- **Risks:** handover delays, finish quality, and competing supply completing at the same time, which can cap rents and resale prices in that area.
- **No rent while you wait:** you keep paying rent until handover, which our calculator does not model automatically. Add it to buying costs if relevant.

## The Golden Visa factor

Owning property worth **at least AED 2 million** can qualify you for the UAE's 10-year Golden Visa, subject to conditions on mortgages, off-plan payments and documentation. For some households, the residency security has real value beyond the numbers, especially self-employed residents and those without employer sponsorship. Do not let it push you into a purchase that fails financially, though: an AED 2M threshold just above your comfortable budget can mean higher service charges and less financial flexibility.

## Decision checklist for Dubai

1. **How long will you stay?** Under three years, rent. Over five years, buying is usually favoured at today's yields.
2. **Is your job secure, and do you have a plan if you leave the UAE?** A property you can rent out from abroad is a fallback, not a guarantee.
3. **Can you fund 20% plus about 7% in fees** and still keep an emergency fund?
4. **What does the building charge?** Check the service charge per square foot and its history.
5. **What happens at the end of your fixed rate?** Run the numbers at a rate 2 percentage points higher.
6. **Compare like with like:** the rent of the exact type of unit you would buy, in the same community.
7. **Stress-test prices:** rerun with 0% growth and a five-year horizon.
