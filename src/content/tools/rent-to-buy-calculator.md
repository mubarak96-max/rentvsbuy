## What does a rent to buy calculator tell you?

**A rent to buy calculator answers two questions. Should you buy a home or keep renting, and if you're renting, how much rent makes the two options equal?** With UK defaults of a £290,000 home, £1,350 rent and a 4.3% mortgage, buying finishes about £106,000 ahead after 10 years. Renting only wins if a comparable home rents for less than about **£794 a month**. If you're on the government's Rent to Buy scheme, the same tool shows how much the discounted rent speeds up your deposit savings.

"Rent to buy" means two different things in the UK, and this page covers both:

1. **The Rent to Buy scheme.** This is a tenancy at a reduced rent, designed to help you save a deposit.
2. **Comparing renting to buying in general.** This includes the classic "how much rent equals a mortgage?" question.

## How to use this calculator for a UK rent or buy decision

The UK preset already reflects how buying works here:

- **No recurring property tax for owners.** Council tax is paid by renters and owners alike, so it's left out of both sides.
- **Buying costs of 2.5%.** This covers conveyancing, surveys, mortgage fees and moving. Stamp Duty Land Tax is zero for first-time buyers up to £300,000 under [HMRC's current rates](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates). First-time buyers pay 5% between £300,001 and £500,000, and there's no relief above £500,000. If you're not a first-time buyer, standard rates on a £290,000 home come to £4,500, so raise this input.
- **A 10% deposit and 25-year term**, which are both common for first-time buyers.
- **Rent growth of 4% against price growth of 3%.** Both are editable and both make a big difference.

To use it, enter the price of the home you'd buy and the rent for a similar one. Set the horizon to how long you'd stay, then read three outputs: the **verdict**, the **break-even year** and the **break-even rent**.

## Worked example: buying vs renting with UK defaults

**Buying.** A 10% deposit on £290,000 is £29,000. Add buying costs of 2.5% (£7,250) and you need **£36,250** up front. The £261,000 mortgage at 4.3% over 25 years works out at about **£1,421 a month** using the standard repayment formula. Add maintenance at 1% a year (about £242 a month) and buildings insurance at 0.15% (about £36), and owning costs roughly **£1,699 a month** at the start.

**Renting.** Rent is £1,350 plus about £10 a month for contents insurance. The renter invests the £36,250 and the roughly £340 a month that renting saves.

**After 10 years.** The home is worth about £390,000 and the mortgage balance is down to about £188,000. After 2% selling costs, the owner's equity is roughly £197,000. The renter's portfolio is about £90,000. **Buying leads by roughly £106,000, and it pulls ahead in year 2.**

Why does buying win so clearly here when it doesn't in the US? Owners pay no recurring property tax. Buying and selling costs are low by international standards. Rents are expected to grow faster than prices. And a 10% deposit means less money misses out on investment returns.

## How to use the break-even rent output

The break-even rent is the monthly rent, in today's money, at which renting and buying finish level at your chosen horizon. It works like a decision line:

- **Your comparable rent is above it:** buying is the better financial choice.
- **Your comparable rent is below it:** renting and investing the difference wins.

It changes a lot with the horizon, because buying and selling costs have to be recovered:

| How long you stay | Break-even rent | Buying wins if comparable rent is above |
|---|---|---|
| 2 years | ~£1,200 | £1,200 |
| 3 years | ~£1,020 | £1,020 |
| 5 years | ~£885 | £885 |
| 10 years | ~£794 | £794 |

With £1,350 rent, buying wins on every horizon from two years up. If you only expect to stay two years and could rent the same home for £1,150, renting would edge it.

### Rent to buy ratio: a quicker check

The **price-to-rent ratio** is the home price divided by annual rent. Here it's £290,000 ÷ £16,200, or about **17.9**. Its inverse, the **gross rental yield**, is about 5.6%. These ratios are useful for comparing towns quickly, but they leave out your mortgage rate, deposit and horizon. The break-even rent includes all of them. Our guide to the [price-to-rent ratio](/blog/price-to-rent-ratio/) explains how to read it.

## The Rent to Buy scheme: how it works

The facts below are from [GOV.UK's Rent to Buy guidance](https://www.gov.uk/rent-to-buy), checked in November 2026.

- **Rent level:** normally **20% below market rent**.
- **Where:** England outside London. London has London Living Rent instead.
- **Tenancy length:** the first tenancy is for **up to 2 years**, and the landlord may extend it if you need more time to save.
- **Who qualifies:** you must be in full-time or part-time work, be a first-time buyer (or returning to ownership after a relationship breakdown), and be able to pay rent and save for a deposit at the same time. Landlords may check your income and credit history.
- **Buying afterwards:** you can use the savings to buy **any home**. If the landlord agrees, you may be able to buy the home you rent at its current market value, possibly through shared ownership.

### Worked example: what Rent to Buy saves you

Say the home would normally rent for £1,350. At 20% off, you'd pay about **£1,080**. That saves £270 a month, or about **£6,480 over a two-year tenancy**, before any interest.

That's a real help, but it's well short of the £36,250 a full purchase needs on the defaults. Most tenants will need the discount *plus* their own regular saving. If you add £600 a month of your own, for £870 a month in total, you'd have about £21,000 after two years. That covers a 10% deposit on a home of around £170,000 with costs, or a larger share through [shared ownership](/shared-ownership-calculator/).

### Should you stay on discounted rent or buy as soon as you can?

Put £1,080 into the calculator as your rent and compare:

| Rent | Horizon | Result |
|---|---|---|
| £1,080 (Rent to Buy) | 2 years | Renting ahead by ~£3,000 |
| £1,080 (Rent to Buy) | 10 years | Buying ahead by ~£55,000 |
| £1,350 (market) | 10 years | Buying ahead by ~£106,000 |

The scheme is worth using for its intended purpose, which is a short period of lower rent while you save. Over a long horizon, buying still comes out well ahead on these assumptions. Don't let a comfortable discounted tenancy drift on longer than you need it.

### Rent to buy mortgage calculator: what will the mortgage cost?

When your Rent to Buy tenancy ends and you apply for a mortgage, the rate you're offered will set your monthly payment for the length of the fix. Here's a £261,000 repayment mortgage over 25 years at different rates:

| Mortgage rate | Monthly repayment |
|---|---|
| 3.8% | ~£1,349 |
| 4.3% (default) | ~£1,421 |
| 4.8% | ~£1,496 |
| 5.3% | ~£1,572 |

Each half a percentage point adds roughly £75 a month. Most UK mortgages are fixed for two or five years and then move to a new deal, so test a higher rate as well as today's quote. If £1,500 a month would stretch you, aim for a cheaper home or a bigger deposit before you leave the scheme.

## Rent and buy decision checklist (UK)

- [ ] **First-time buyer?** Check whether Stamp Duty relief applies to your price.
- [ ] **Leasehold flat?** Add ground rent and service charges to the monthly fees input.
- [ ] **Rent to Buy tenant?** Use your discounted rent for the short-term view and market rent for the long-term one.
- [ ] **Mortgage rate:** use your broker's quote, including the rate you'd move to when a two- or five-year fix ends.
- [ ] **Horizon:** if you could move within three years, check the three-year break-even rent.
- [ ] **Price growth:** test 1% a year. On the defaults, buying still leads by about £43,000 after 10 years, so the UK case holds up in a slow market.
