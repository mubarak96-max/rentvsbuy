## How do you use the rent vs buy calculator in NZ?

**On our New Zealand defaults, renting and investing finishes slightly ahead of buying after 10 years, by about NZ$25,000 on an NZ$800,000 home. That is close enough that your own rent, interest rate and price-growth assumptions decide the answer.** Buying generally wins if comparable homes rent for more than about NZ$650 a week, if you fix below 5%, or if prices grow faster than 3.5% a year.

New Zealand rents are quoted weekly, so convert before you enter them: weekly rent × 52 ÷ 12 = monthly rent. NZ$620 a week is about NZ$2,690 a month. The calculator simulates every month. The buyer pays the deposit and legal costs up front, then the mortgage, council rates, maintenance and insurance. The renter pays rent plus contents insurance and invests both the deposit and any monthly saving. Our [methodology page](/methodology/) explains the comparison.

## Worked example: NZ$800,000 house vs NZ$620 a week

**Upfront cash:** NZ$160,000 deposit plus NZ$8,000 in costs, or **NZ$168,000 in total**. Many first-home buyers fund part of this from KiwiSaver.

**Mortgage payment:** the loan is NZ$640,000 at 5% over 30 years. The amortisation formula, M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n) with r = 0.05 ÷ 12 and n = 360, gives **about NZ$3,436 a month**, or roughly NZ$793 a week. In the first month, NZ$2,667 is interest and only NZ$769 is principal.

**Total monthly cost of owning in year 1:** about NZ$4,569. That is the mortgage plus about NZ$1,133 for rates, maintenance and insurance. The renter pays NZ$2,710 including contents insurance and invests the NZ$1,859 monthly gap.

**After 10 years:**

- Buyer's net worth: about **NZ$574,000** (home worth about NZ$1.13 million, less 3% selling costs and a remaining loan of about NZ$521,000)
- Renter's portfolio: about **NZ$600,000**
- Renter ahead by about **NZ$25,600**, with **no break-even year within 10 years**
- **Break-even rent: about NZ$2,830 a month (about NZ$650 a week).** If comparable homes rent for more than that, buying tends to win.

The quick 5% rule gives NZ$800,000 × 5% ÷ 12 = NZ$3,333 a month (about NZ$770 a week). It flags renting as cheaper, but the full model shows the gap is smaller than that shortcut suggests. Try it in the [5% rule calculator](/5-percent-rule-calculator/).

The New Zealand result is very sensitive to price growth. With a 6.5% investment return against 3.5% appreciation, renting edges ahead. Narrow that gap and buying pulls ahead.

## What does it cost to buy and sell in New Zealand?

| Cost | Typical amount | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Stamp duty or transfer tax | None | n/a |
| Conveyancing (lawyer) | Often NZ$1,500–NZ$2,500 | Buyer |
| Building inspection report | Often NZ$500–NZ$1,000 | Buyer |
| LIM report from council | A few hundred dollars | Buyer |
| Registered valuation | Often required by the bank | Buyer |
| Real estate agent commission | Commonly 2.5%–4% plus GST, often tiered | Seller |
| Marketing and legal on sale | A few thousand dollars | Seller |

Because buying costs are so low, New Zealand is one of the few markets where buying can make sense over shorter horizons, provided the monthly numbers work. The large cost comes at the other end, when you pay the agent on sale.

## What are the mortgage rules in New Zealand?

**Loan-to-value ratio (LVR) restrictions.** The Reserve Bank of New Zealand limits high-LVR lending with "speed limits". From 1 December 2025, banks may make up to 25% of new owner-occupier lending at an LVR above 80% (less than a 20% deposit). For investors, up to 10% of lending may be above 70% LVR. Low-deposit loans exist but are rationed, and they usually carry higher rates or fees.

**Debt-to-income (DTI) restrictions.** Since July 2024, no more than 20% of new owner-occupier lending can go to borrowers with debt above six times gross annual income. For investors the threshold is seven times. On our defaults, a NZ$640,000 loan needs a household income of about NZ$107,000 to stay within 6×. The Reserve Bank's October 2025 review left DTI settings unchanged. From 2026, a new Financial Policy Committee oversees both tools.

**The Official Cash Rate (OCR).** The OCR was cut sharply through 2024 and 2025, raised in July and August 2026, and then held at **2.75%** at the Reserve Bank's 28 October 2026 review, according to the [Reserve Bank of New Zealand](https://www.rbnz.govt.nz/hub/home). Most New Zealand mortgages are fixed for one to five years, so your rate resets several times over a 30-year loan. Enter an average rate for your horizon and test a higher one.

**Kāinga Ora First Home Loan.** Eligible buyers can borrow through participating banks with as little as a 5% deposit, under income and price caps.

## How does KiwiSaver fit into rent vs buy?

The KiwiSaver first-home withdrawal is the biggest source of deposits for New Zealand first-home buyers. According to [Kāinga Ora](https://kaingaora.govt.nz/home-ownership/kiwisaver-first-home-withdrawal/):

- you generally need at least **three years** of KiwiSaver membership
- you must leave at least **NZ$1,000** in your account
- the property must be your home and you must not currently own property, although "second chance" applicants may qualify
- you can make only one first-home withdrawal

The First Home Grant closed to new applications in 2024, so the withdrawal itself is now the main help available.

For the calculator, treat KiwiSaver money in your deposit like any other savings. If you rent instead, that balance stays invested in your fund, which is exactly what the renter's opportunity-cost line models. If your fund is conservative, lower the investment return to reflect what that money would really earn.

## Which New Zealand taxes affect owners?

- **No capital gains tax on your main home.** The bright-line test applies to residential property sold within **two years** of purchase if bought on or after 1 July 2024, and the main-home exclusion usually removes owner-occupiers from it. It is mainly relevant if you rented the place out, or never lived in it, before selling.
- **Council rates** are charged by your district or city council based on the property's rating valuation. Many councils have raised rates by double-digit percentages in recent years to pay for water and infrastructure. Use your council's rates search to find the real figure, and divide it by the price to get the percentage.
- **Insurance** includes a levy for the Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake (formerly EQC). Premiums are higher in flood-prone and seismic areas, and some properties are hard to insure at all. Get a quote before you go unconditional.
- **No mortgage interest deduction** for owner-occupiers, which matches the calculator.

## When does renting win in New Zealand, and when does buying win?

**Renting tends to win when:**

- comparable rent is below about NZ$650 a week for an NZ$800,000 home (scale this to your price)
- you are likely to move within five to seven years, when the agent's commission on sale hurts
- you would be borrowing close to six times your income, with no buffer if rates reset higher
- insurance or rates for the property are unusually high

**Buying tends to win when:**

- you can fix at a rate below about 5% and expect prices to grow at least 4% a year
- you plan to stay 10 years or more
- you can use KiwiSaver plus savings to reach 20%, avoiding low-equity premiums
- rents in your area are high relative to prices, as in parts of Christchurch and the regions

## NZ rent vs buy checklist

1. **Convert weekly rent to monthly** (× 52 ÷ 12) for the same type of home.
2. **Look up the council rates** for the property and enter them as a percentage of the price.
3. **Get an insurance quote** that includes natural hazard cover before you trust the result.
4. **Check your KiwiSaver withdrawal** eligibility and how much it adds to your deposit.
5. **Confirm you are under 6× DTI** and whether you need a low-deposit exemption.
6. **Test two rates:** your fixed offer, and that rate plus 1 percentage point for future refixes.
7. **Budget for maintenance**, especially on older weatherboard homes and anything built in the 1990s–2000s with monolithic cladding.
8. **Compare with the break-even rent**, and rent if comparable homes cost less than that.
