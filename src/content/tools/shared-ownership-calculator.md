## Is shared ownership better than renting?

**Often, yes, on the numbers. On our UK defaults, a 40% share of a £290,000 home costs about the same each month as renting it, needs only about £14,500 upfront, and leaves you roughly £63,000 better off after 10 years.** But shared ownership comes with costs and restrictions that renting doesn't have: you pay 100% of repairs, service charges can rise, you hold a lease rather than a freehold, and selling can be slower. This shared ownership calculator shows the financial case. The rest of this page covers the parts a calculator can't.

Shared ownership is a UK scheme in which you buy a share of a home, usually from a housing association, and pay rent to the landlord on the rest. You buy between 10% and 75% of the full market value, and the deposit is usually 5% to 10% of the share you're buying.

## How to use the shared ownership calculator

1. **Enter the full market value** of the home, not the price of your share.
2. **Choose your share.** It can be between 10% and 75%. New leases allow shares as low as 10%.
3. **Set the rent on the unowned share.** The default is 2.75% a year. GOV.UK allows up to 3% for new-build homes, and a resale home keeps the rent the previous owner paid.
4. **Add the service charge** from the home's key information document to the HOA / service charge input. This calculator preloads £150 a month, which we use in the example below; replace it with the real figure.
5. **Enter a comparable rent** for renting a similar home outright.

The calculator puts a mortgage on your share only. Rent on the landlord's share rises each year in line with rent growth. **You pay 100% of maintenance and service charges**, worked out on the whole home's value. When you sell, you receive your share of the home's value minus your mortgage and selling costs.

## Worked example: 25% vs 40% share vs renting vs buying outright

The example uses the UK defaults: a £290,000 home, a 4.3% mortgage over 25 years, a 10% deposit on the share, 2.5% buying costs on the share, 3% annual price growth, 4% annual rent growth, and comparable rent of £1,350 a month. We've added a £150 monthly service charge to every ownership option.

| First month | Rent | 25% share | 40% share | Buy 100% (10% deposit) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | £0 | ~£9,060 | ~£14,500 | ~£36,250 |
| Mortgage | — | ~£355 | ~£569 | ~£1,421 |
| Rent (market or on the unowned share) | £1,350 | ~£498 | ~£399 | — |
| Maintenance + insurance | — | ~£278 | ~£278 | ~£278 |
| Service charge | — | £150 | £150 | £150 |
| Contents insurance | £10 | — | — | — |
| **Monthly total** | **~£1,360** | **~£1,282** | **~£1,395** | **~£1,849** |

**How the 40% figures are worked out.** Your share is £116,000. A 10% deposit is £11,600, which leaves a £104,400 mortgage. At 4.3% over 25 years, the repayment formula gives about £569 a month. The landlord's 60% is worth £174,000, and 2.75% of that is £4,785 a year, or £399 a month.

**After 10 years**, with the renter investing whatever cash they don't spend on housing:

| Option | Net worth after 10 years | Versus renting |
|---|---|---|
| 25% share | ~£75,000 | ~£59,000 ahead |
| 40% share | ~£90,000 | ~£63,000 ahead |
| Buy 100% | ~£194,000 | ~£78,000 ahead |

Shared ownership wins here because it gets you exposure to house prices with very little cash. Only £14,500 is tied up, so the renter's investment advantage is small. Meanwhile your 40% share grows with the whole market. With a £290,000 home growing at 3% a year, your share rises from £116,000 to about £156,000.

## When does renting beat shared ownership?

The case for shared ownership is sensitive to a few inputs. Here are single changes to the 40% example, measured over 10 years:

| Change | Shared owner's lead over renting |
|---|---|
| Example as above | ~£63,000 |
| Price growth 1% a year | ~£40,000 |
| Service charge £250 a month | ~£44,000 |
| Comparable rent £1,100 instead of £1,350 | ~£15,000 |
| 5-year horizon | ~£22,000 |
| Investment return 8% | ~£58,000 |

Shared ownership is still ahead in every one of these model runs. But the margin narrows fast when comparable rents are cheap or service charges are high. Remember too that the model **doesn't include**:

- **Rising service charges and major works bills.** These are set by the landlord or managing agent and are hard to forecast. Ask for the past five years of charges.
- **Staircasing costs.** Each step involves a valuation, legal fees and admin fees.
- **Resale friction.** A 4 to 12 week nomination period, a smaller pool of eligible buyers, and lenders that may be less keen on low-share resales.
- **Lease issues.** You're a leaseholder. Check the length of the lease and any restrictions on subletting or alterations.
- **Mortgage choice.** Fewer lenders offer shared ownership mortgages, so compare rates with a broker.

If you think it's likely you'll need to sell quickly, put more weight on these risks than on the calculator's 10-year number.

## Shared ownership rules in 2026: what to check

The points below are from [GOV.UK's shared ownership guidance](https://www.gov.uk/shared-ownership-scheme), checked in November 2026.

- **Eligibility.** Household income must be **£80,000 or less outside London, or £90,000 or less in London**. You must be unable to afford a suitable home outright and fit one of the listed buyer types, such as a first-time buyer or a former owner. Some homes also require a local connection.
- **Share size.** You can buy 10% to 75% at the start. The usual range is 25% to 75%.
- **Rent.** For new-build homes, rent is capped at **3% of the landlord's share** a year, and most landlords charge **2.75%**. For leases from 12 October 2023, the lease sets the annual increase at either RPI plus up to 0.5% or CPI plus 1%. Rent can go up at review but never down.
- **Staircasing.** Most leases allow purchases of 10% or more. Some newer leases allow 5%. If you bought on or after 1 April 2021, you can usually buy 1% a year for the first 15 years at an HPI-based price. You can usually reach 100%, but the cap is 80% in designated protected areas.
- **Repairs.** You pay for repairs whatever share you own. Some newer leases have a 10-year initial repair period, during which the landlord covers essential external and structural repairs, plus a repairs allowance that is usually £500 a year.
- **Selling.** The landlord has a **4, 8 or 12 week** nomination period before you can sell on the open market, unless you own 100%.

Stamp Duty works differently for shared ownership. You can make a **market value election**, which means paying once on the full market value and nothing more as you staircase. Or you can pay in stages, with nothing more due until your share goes above 80%. First-time buyer relief may apply to your first share. See [HMRC's guidance on SDLT for shared ownership](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/shared-ownership-property) and ask your conveyancer which option suits you.

## Rent to Buy vs shared ownership

| | Rent to Buy | Shared ownership |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | A tenancy at about 20% below market rent | Part-ownership of the home |
| Typical length | Up to 2 years at first | Indefinite, with staircasing optional |
| Upfront cash | Usual tenancy deposit | 5–10% of your share, plus legal costs |
| Repairs | Landlord | You pay 100% |
| Exposure to house prices | None | On your share |
| Where | England outside London | England (other UK nations run their own schemes) |

If you need time to build savings, Rent to Buy is lower risk. If you have a small deposit and plan to stay at least five years, shared ownership usually builds more wealth, as long as the service charge and resale terms are acceptable.
